# Why Brother

You want to give an agent useful work and get on with your day. Full auto asks for trust you may not have earned yet. Watching every action consumes the attention delegation was supposed to free. Brother exists for the gap between an agent's ability to produce work and your ability to leave it alone responsibly.

The proposed answer is bounded delegation: agree what may change, inspect the active controls, require evidence for the result, and keep consequential decisions human. Surveillance can reveal a mistake; it is not itself an enforced boundary or proof of correctness.

Summaries compress away what senior reviewers need: real scope, the check that discriminated the change, the untested edge, semantic definition, rollback path, source of the expected result, and the decision nobody automated away.

Brother therefore optimizes for **verified useful work**, not maximum autonomous output:

- trivial work gets no ceremony;
- substantial work gets bounded execution and receipts;
- risky work gets stronger evidence;
- missing evidence is `NO-DATA`;
- memory remains context;
- human acceptance remains explicit;
- professional knowledge loads as a lens rather than a menu of modes.

The goal is not to slow every change. Spend assurance where a wrong answer is expensive; stay out of the way where reversibility makes ceremony irrational.

The Vault closes the continuity loop. A useful failure lesson can inform the next task at the point of work instead of making you repeat it in every conversation. Recalled context must still survive contact with current code and evidence. Memory is not proof, and accumulating notes does not demonstrate fewer repeat failures.

Confidence should grow from representative runs, recoverable failures, and disclosed unknowns. Receipts make that history inspectable; they do not establish independent certification or a measured reduction in supervision. Those require separate evaluation.
